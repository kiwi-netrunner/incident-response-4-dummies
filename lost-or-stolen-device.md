# Lost or Stolen Device Incident Response Playbook

# Purpose

The purpose of this playbook is to help quickly secure your accounts and personal data if your device is lost or stolen. Acting fast reduces the risk of identity theft, financial loss, and unauthorised access.

## Confirm the situation

Before taking action, quickly consider:

- When and where you last had the device
- Whether it might simply be misplaced nearby
- Whether someone else could safely retrieve it for you

If there is a reasonable chance it is lost or stolen, proceed immediately.

## Try to locate the device

**Use official tracking tools:**

- **Apple devices:** Use Find My on another Apple device or webversion 
- **Android:** Use Find Hub on another Android device or webversion
- **Windows laptop:** Use Find my device using you Microsoft account

**Actions you can take:**

- Play a sound (if nearby)
- Check last known location
- Mark as lost (if available)

If the device appears in an unfamiliar location or is offline unexpectedly, assume theft.

## If the device was stolen while unlocked (urgent escalation)

If your phone or laptop was taken while it was unlocked, assume the attacker may already have access to your apps, emails, messages, and saved passwords. Treat this as a high-risk identity compromise, not just a lost device.

**Immediat actions:**

- Start account security from a safe device
- Secure your primary email account first. Change the password immediately and sign out of all active sessions. 
- Secure high-impact accounts in this order:
1. Banking and payment apps (freeze cards if possible)
2. Email and cloud storage accounts
3. Messaging apps (Signal, Telegram, etc.)
4. Social media accounts
- Revoke all active sessions and trusted devices. Look for options like “log out of all devices” or “remove trusted devices”.
- If available, use remote lock/wipe the stolen device immediately. 

## Lock or wipe the device remotely

Do not delay this step if the device was stolen while it was unlocked, sensitive data (photos, emails, documents, work access) may be exposed. 

**If possible, take these actions:**

- **Lock device remotely** (prevents access)
- Display a message with contact details (optional)
- **Erase device data** if you are confident it won’t be recovered

## Secure your most important accounts first

Start with your **primary account (e.g. Apple ID)**, because it is the gateway to everything else.

**Do the following:**

- Change the password immediately
- Sign out of all devices/sessions
- Enable or confirm multi-factor authentication (MFA)
- Remove the stolen device from the trusted devices

**Then secure:**

- Banking and payment apps
- Social media accounts
- Cloud storage (e.g. photos, files, backups)

## Contact your mobile network provider (phone only)

**If a phone is lost or stolen:**

- Ask them to **block your SIM card immediately**
- Request a replacement SIM
- Check for unusual activity on your account

This helps prevent SIM swap attacks and unauthorised calls/messages.

## Check for suspicious activity

**In the following hours and days, monitor:**

- Bank transactions
- Email login alerts
- Password reset emails you did not request
- Messages sent from your accounts

**If anything looks suspicious:**

- Change passwords
- Report fraud to your bank immediately

## Report the loss or theft

**Depending on severity:**

- Report to local police (especially if theft is likely or sensitive data was stored)
- Keep a record of:
	-  Device model and serial number
  	- Time and location of loss
  	- Any tracking information

## Protect yourself from follow-on attacks

Even after securing accounts:

- Be alert to phishing emails pretending to be “device recovery” or “account security”
- Do not approve unexpected login prompts
- Watch for impersonation attempts using your data

## Recover and rebuild access

Once the situation is under control:

- Restore a new or replacement device from a secure backup
- Review which accounts had MFA enabled (enable everywhere possible)
- Update any reused or weak passwords
- Consider using a password manager going forward
