# Phishing Incident Response Playbook

# Purpose

The purpose of this playbook is to explains what to do if you receive a suspicious email or text message, phone call or if you think you may have interacted with one.

## What is Phishing?

Phishing is when a threat actor pretends to be a trusted person or organisation to trick you into:

- Revealing your passwords, personal or banking information
- Making payments or transferring money
- Opening malicious attachments
- Clicking harmful links
- Installing software or apps

Common examples include messages claiming to be from:

- Banks
- Delivery companies
- Government departments
- Utility providers
- Employers
- Colleagues
- Friends or family members

Other forms of phishing may be carried out via voice or video calls.

## How to Recognise a Phishing Message / Call

**Be cautious if a message:**

- Creates urgency or panic
- Demands immediate action
- Offers something that seems too good to be true
- Requests passwords, security codes, or banking details
- Contains unexpected attachments
- Includes links that do not match the sender
- Comes from an unfamiliar sender
- Contains spelling or grammatical mistakes

When in doubt, treat the message as suspicious.

## Incident Classification

### Situation A: Received a suspicious message but did not interact with it or clicked a link but did not enter any information

**Examples:**

- You received a suspicious email.
- You received a suspicious text message.
- You did not click any links.
- You did not open attachments.
- You did not reply.

**Actions:**

1. Do not interact with the message.
2. Block the sender if appropriate.

**Outcome:**

Did not interact: No further action is normally required.
Clicked the link but no information entered: Risk is generally low but should still be reported.

### Situation B: Entered credentials or personal information

**Examples:**

- Entered a password.
- Entered banking information.
- Entered personal details.
- Entered a one-time passcode.

**Actions:**

1. Change the affected password.
2. Change the password on any other accounts using the same password.
3. Enable multi-factor authentication (MFA) if available.
4. Contact your bank if financial information was disclosed.
5. Monitor accounts for suspicious activity.

**Outcome:**

This is considered a significant security incident and requires immediate action.


### Situation C: Opened an attachment or installed an app or software

**Examples:**

- Opened an attachment from an unexpected or suspicious sender.
- Opened a document that asked you to enable additional features or permissions.
- Downloaded and installed an app from a link in an email or text message.
- Allowed a downloaded file to run on your device.

**Actions:**

1. Disconnect the device from the internet by turning off Wi-Fi and mobile data.
2. Stop using the device for sensitive activities such as online banking, shopping, or accessing important accounts.
3. Run a security scan using reputable antivirus or security software if available.
4. Change passwords for important accounts from a different, trusted device, especially if you entered any credentials after opening the attachment or app.
5. Monitor your bank accounts, email accounts, and other important services for unusual activity.
6. If the device continues to behave strangely (for example, running slowly, displaying unexpected pop-ups, or installing unknown software), seek assistance from a trusted technical support service or a knowledgeable friend or family member.

**Outcome:**

There is a risk that malicious software may have been installed on the device. Taking prompt action can help limit the impact and protect your accounts and personal information.


# Reporting Procedure (if applicable)

You may want to report phishing incident to your bank or service provider. 
When reporting a phishing incident, provide:

- Your name
- Date and time of the incident
- How the message was received (email or SMS)
- Sender details
- Subject line
- What actions you took
- Whether any information was entered
- Screenshots if available

**Example:**

"I received a text message claiming to be from my bank at approximately 14:30. I clicked the link but did not enter any information. I closed the page immediately and am reporting the incident for review."

# What Not to Do

**Do not:**

- Reply to suspicious messages
- Click links to "verify" information
- Open unexpected attachments
- Provide passwords or MFA codes
- Forward suspicious messages
- Assume a familiar sender is genuine
