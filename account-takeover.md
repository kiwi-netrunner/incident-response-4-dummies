# Account Takeover Incident Response Playbook

# Purpose

The purpose of this playbook is to explains what to do if someone gains unauthorised access to your online account. It is designed for people who may not have a technical background.

An account takeover may involve someone accessing your email, social media, banking, work, shopping, or other online accounts without your permission.

## What is an Account Takeover?

An account takeover occurs when another person gains access to your account by:

- Stealing or guessing your password
- Tricking you into revealing login credentials or details
- Using information obtained from a data breach
- Taking control of your email account and resetting passwords
- Bypassing security controls

## Common Warning Signs

You may be experiencing an account takeover if you notice:

- You cannot log in with your usual password
- You receive password reset emails that you did not request
- Unrecognised logins or security alerts
- Changes to your account details, such as your email address or phone number
- Messages, posts, or purchases that you did not make
- Friends or colleagues reporting suspicious activity from your account
- Missing or altered information

Treat any of these signs as a potential security incident.

## Immediate Actions

### Step 1: Secure the Account

If you can still access the account:

1. Change your password immediately.
2. Sign out of all active sessions or devices if the option is available.
3. Enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA).
4. Review recovery email addresses and phone numbers.
5. Remove any details you do not recognise.

If you cannot access the account:

1. Use the account recovery process provided by the service.
2. Contact the service provider's support team.
3. Follow their identity verification process.

### Step 2: Secure Your Email Account

Email accounts are often used to reset passwords for other services.

- Change your email password immediately.
- Enable MFA if it is not already enabled.
- Check for unauthorised forwarding rules or recovery settings.

### Step 4: Report the Incident

Notify the appropriate contact as soon as possible.

- Report the issue to the service provider.
- Contact your bank if financial information may be affected.

## Containment Actions

The goal of containment is to prevent further misuse.

### Check Other Accounts

If the same password was used elsewhere:

- Change those passwords immediately.

### Review Account Activity

**Look for:**

- Unrecognised logins
- Changes to profile information
- New devices connected to the account
- Messages or transactions you did not authorise

### Warn Affected Contacts

**If suspicious messages were sent from your account:**

- Inform friends, colleagues, customers, or contacts.
- Advise them not to click links or open attachments from the suspicious messages.

## Recovery Actions

### Restore Account Settings

**Review and correct:**

- Email addresses
- Phone numbers
- Security questions
- MFA settings
- Payment details

## Check for Further Compromise

**Monitor the account for several days and watch for:**

- Additional login alerts
- Password reset attempts
- Unauthorised changes

## Post-Incident Review

After recovery:

1. Identify how the account was compromised.
2. Review whether MFA was enabled.
3. Determine whether passwords were reused.
